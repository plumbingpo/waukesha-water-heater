Upfront Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Waukesha Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, handling everything from a failed heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

Most water heater failures send small signals well before the tank gives out. Keep an eye out for hot water with a rusty tint, popping noises when the burner fires, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually a thick layer of sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a worn dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than cleaning up after a failed tank.

Not every call is an emergency. Many homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), replacing on your own schedule beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your unit has left.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the idea behind our maintenance plans. Once a year, we drain and flush the tank, measure the anode rod, test the relief valve, inspect the venting and gas connections or the electrical components, and run a descaling cycle on tankless units, all tailored to the equipment in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, forces the burner to run longer, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your equipment's age, your household's hot water demands, and your local water.

Consider maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already bought. Each visit helps keep the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve in working order, which lowers what you pay to heat water while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most households, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Waukesha Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute to the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water needs. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely holds out after two teenagers start showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver continuous hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line sizing, electrical capacity, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Waukesha

Most service calls we get come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The hot water runs out fast, the tank pops every time the burner fires, or the tankless unit locks out with an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. Our technicians handle all of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that keeps discharging is warning you that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can burn a child in seconds. A gas smell near the heater means leave the house first and call the gas utility from outside, then call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where pressure is spiking, and leak repair on fittings, drain valves, and relief lines.
