How Wauwatosa Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many early failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Whatever type of water heater your Wauwatosa home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units lose heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
